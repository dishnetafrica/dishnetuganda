How to Start a WiFi Hotspot Business in Uganda

In hundreds of Ugandan trading centres, people pay heavily for slow mobile data while a single good internet connection could serve the whole street cheaper. That gap is a business — and it's one ordinary shop owners are already running profitably with DishNet.

How the model works

One strong connection (Starlink upcountry, fiber in cities) feeds weatherproof access points covering your premises or street. A captive portal sells time bundles — an hour, a day, a week — paid instantly by MTN MoMo or Airtel Money. Customers get internet far cheaper than mobile data; you keep the revenue; the system runs itself.

What it takes to start

Three ingredients decide success: location (foot traffic within 50–100 metres — arcades, stages, markets, hostels), a fair price (undercut mobile data meaningfully and volume follows), and uptime (a zone that's down for a weekend loses its reputation for a month). The technical stack — backhaul, access points, portal, billing — is our job, delivered as a working package with training.

What does it earn?

We won't print fantasy figures: earnings depend entirely on footfall and pricing. What we can say is that busy-location owners routinely recover their setup investment within months, and that we'll model your specific location's numbers with you honestly before you spend anything — including telling you if we think the spot won't work.

Why not build it yourself?

You can buy a router and share a password — until the connection chokes, nobody pays, and the neighbour's kid streams all night for free. The managed system exists to solve exactly that: per-user bandwidth control, automatic payments, usage caps, and monitoring. You run a business, not an IT department.
