Running Starlink on Solar Power in Uganda: A Practical Guide

Half of the Starlink installations we do in Uganda are beyond reliable grid power — farms, lodges, schools, field sites. The good news: Starlink and solar are a natural pair. Here's what actually matters.

How much power does Starlink use?

A Standard kit typically draws around 50–75W while active — roughly like a laptop and a bright bulb together. The Starlink Mini uses far less, often 25–40W, which is why it's become the darling of field teams. Over a full day, a Standard kit consumes roughly 1.2–1.8 kWh.

What a solid solar setup looks like

For round-the-clock internet, we usually size systems around three components: panels of about 400–600W to harvest enough even on cloudy days, a lithium battery of 2–3 kWh usable capacity to carry the dish through the night, and a quality inverter or DC solution to feed the kit cleanly. Undersizing the battery is the classic mistake — the system works beautifully for a week, then a string of cloudy days kills the internet at 4am.

Mistakes we see (and fix)

Cheap inverters whose dirty power damages equipment; batteries shared with heavy appliances like irons and freezers that flatten them by midnight; panels mounted flat, collecting dust and losing harvest; and no surge protection in areas with fierce lightning seasons. Every one of these is avoidable with a proper design.

Running only in business hours?

If you only need internet during the day — a shop, a station office — you can go smaller: the dish drinks directly from solar while the sun shines, with a modest battery buffer. That cuts system cost significantly.

We design and install the power side together with the dish, sized for your actual usage and budget. Talk to us before buying panels — ten minutes of advice routinely saves people from buying the wrong system twice.
