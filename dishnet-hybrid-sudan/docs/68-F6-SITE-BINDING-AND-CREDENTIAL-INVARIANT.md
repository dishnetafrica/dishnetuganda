## Part 1 — Invariant C1: what "generated credentials" means

docs/67 decided that the portal receives generated AAA credentials rather than
the voucher code. §3.1, §3.2 and §6 there describe the properties. This states
them as a **binding invariant**, because the failure mode is not a bug — it is a
later simplification that looks like a cleanup.

> **C1.** The AAA credential is generated by the publisher and is independent of
> the commercial voucher in every respect.
>
> 1. the AAA **username** is not derived from the voucher code, the customer,
>    `radius_ref`, the site, the batch, or any commercial identifier;
> 2. the AAA **password** is not derived from any of those, and **not from the
>    username**;
> 3. both come from a CSPRNG;
> 4. the voucher code is never written to `radcheck`, `radreply`, `radacct` or
>    `radpostauth`, in any column, in any form;
> 5. the credential's lifetime is the voucher's usable window and no longer.

### Why it will be attacked

The pressure is real and will sound reasonable. `username = password = code`
(docs/33 §416–417) is one line shorter, needs no generation, no storage, no
uniqueness check, and makes support easier: a guest quotes their code and staff
can look up the `radcheck` row directly. Every one of those is true, and the
reason they lose is one measurement — docs/65 §12.4 — that the password is
written verbatim to `radpostauth` on **both** authentication outcomes. Reverting
C1 puts the commercial instrument, including codes typed by mistake, into a
readable table for the life of the deployment.

Anyone proposing that change is not wrong about the convenience. They are
missing one fact, and it is in docs/65 §12.4.

### How it is held

Written rules erode. This one gets a guard at implementation time, in the style
the codebase already uses — `tests/test_definer_roles.php` asserts that nothing
under `src/` or `bin/` mentions `Database::inspector()`. The analogue:

* a test asserting no code path derives an AAA password from a voucher code or
  from the username;
* a test asserting a published `radcheck.username` never equals, contains, or is
  a transformation of the voucher code;
* both named so a failure says *why* the rule exists, not just that it broke.

### Deliberately left to implementation

Exact format and alphabet; exact lifetime beyond "the voucher's window";
regeneration on retry (docs/66 §2.6's delete-then-insert may re-mint or reuse —
undecided); storage form in the control plane. None of these is settled by
docs/67, and none may be settled in a way that weakens C1.

## Part 2 — Decision 2, and two premises that do not hold

### 2.1 Correction: the control plane cannot resolve a NAS to a site

Measured:

| Question | Result |
|---|---|
| Columns anywhere holding a NAS identifier | **`mt_sessions.nas_identifier` only** |
| `mt_sites` columns | `id, customer_id, service_id, name, location, created_at` — **no NAS identifier** |
| `mt_devices` columns | includes `site_id`, `serial`, `wg_pubkey`, `tunnel_ip` — **no NAS identifier** |
| Code resolving a NAS identifier to a device, site or customer | **none** in `src/` or `migrations/` |

`mt_sessions.nas_identifier` is written by accounting ingestion and **read by
nothing**.

My prototype invented the mapping: `tools/audit/proto_f6.sql:25` declares
`pf_sites(..., nas_identifier text UNIQUE)` and line 97 resolves the presented
NAS through it. **Every A/B/C result in docs/64 §4 and docs/65 §5 rests on a
lookup that does not exist in production.**

The comparison itself survives — it is still a valid comparison of three
*policies*. What does not survive is any implication that the machinery to
enforce them is present. It is not, under any of the three.

### 2.2 Correction: a portal-supplied NAS is client-asserted

docs/67 §4 has the portal send `POST /portal/redeem { "code": …, "nas": … }`.
That `nas` arrives from the guest's browser.

In docs/65 §9.3 I wrote that the NAS is *"the most trustworthy dimension
available — asserted by infrastructure DishNet provisioned, not by the guest."*
**That is true of a RADIUS packet and false of a portal POST.** The same value
reaches the system by two paths with opposite trust properties, and I treated
them as one. A router asserts its identity to FreeRADIUS over a shared-secret
channel; a browser asserts whatever it is told to.

This is the project's own invariant turned on my own design: *do not allow
caller-supplied identity to become authority.*

Two consequences:

* **site binding cannot be enforced at redemption** from a client-supplied NAS;
* **per-NAS rate limiting is evadable** by varying the claimed NAS. docs/65
  §9.3 made per-NAS the *primary* dimension on the strength of the same wrong
  claim. That is an input to decision 5, recorded here, not re-decided.

### 2.3 Finding: the cross-tenant floor is not enforced where it matters

docs/64 §4 and docs/65 §5 both state that the cross-customer case is refused
under all three binding options. **That is true of redemption and says nothing
about authentication.**

Once a credential is published:

* `radcheck` has no customer, site or NAS column — measured, docs/65 §12.3;
* it has no foreign key to anything — measured, same;
* one FreeRADIUS instance serves every NAS, reading them all from the `nas`
  table, `read_clients = yes` — measured, docs/65 §12.4.

So as far as the AAA schema is concerned, a credential published for customer
P's guest is valid at customer Q's router. And a guest does not need the DishNet
portal to try it: MikroTik's native HotSpot login form takes a username and
password directly.

**One thing is unmeasured** and bounds this finding: whether the SQL authorize
query scopes on anything beyond the username. The schema cannot scope it; the
query might. The standard FreeRADIUS query does not, but this gate does not
answer such questions from remembered defaults — docs/65 §12.4 is the record of
what that costs. **One read-only command closes it:**

```bash
docker exec dn-phase0-radius grep -n -B2 -A 12 \
  'authorize_check_query\|authorize_reply_query' \
  /etc/freeradius/mods-config/sql/main/postgresql/queries.conf
```

Until it is run, §2.4 is stated as a requirement rather than as a repair of a
proven hole.

### 2.4 Decision 2a — the tenant restriction (SECURITY, decided)

> **Every published credential carries a check item restricting it to the NAS
> set of the customer that owns the voucher.**

This is not one of A, B or C. It sits beneath all three, it is not a product
choice, and it is the only place the cross-tenant floor can actually stand:
enforcement must live where the NAS identity is trustworthy, which is the RADIUS
packet, not the portal POST (§2.2).

It also needs no client input at all. The owning customer comes from the
voucher, which the control plane already holds.

Which attribute carries it — `NAS-Identifier`, `NAS-IP-Address`,
`Called-Station-Id`, a huntgroup — is a FreeRADIUS question for the
implementation gate, and depends on §2.3's measurement.

### 2.5 Decision 2b — A, B or C (PRODUCT, open)

With §2.2 applied, the three options reorder by what each must trust:

| | Binding | Input needed | Trust | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **C** | any NAS of the owning customer | **none beyond the voucher** | fully trustworthy — it *is* 2a | lowest; no per-site attribution, ever |
| **A** | the site named on the batch | **none beyond the voucher** — the site is on the voucher already | fully trustworthy | needs a site → NAS-set mapping |
| **B** | the first site actually used | a binding event | trustworthy **only** if pinned on the first successful *authentication* (NAS from RADIUS), never on the portal's claim | highest; plus a window between publication and pinning in which the credential is customer-wide |

The earlier framing had B as a middle option. Under the trust analysis it is the
most expensive, because the only honest binding event is one the control plane
learns about after the fact.

**None of the three is chosen here.** The security floor is 2a and is identical
under all three; what remains is genuinely a business question about how
DishNet's customers run their sites.

### 2.6 The question that settles it

I cannot answer these from the repository, and they decide 2b:

1. **Do DishNet's MikroTik customers typically operate more than one site?**
2. **Is per-site revenue attribution required, or is per-customer sufficient?**
3. **Is voucher stock printed centrally, or per site?**

If the answer to 1 is "mostly no", then A, B and C are indistinguishable in
practice for most of the estate, 2a already carries the security, and **C is
correct on simplicity alone** — it needs no mapping that does not exist. If
multi-site customers are common and 2 matters, A is the only option that gives
attribution from issue.

### 2.7 Precondition for 2a, A and B alike

**A NAS identifier must exist in the control plane and map to a device.** It
does not (§2.1). Concretely, the implementation gate needs:

* a NAS-identifier column on `mt_devices` — which already carries `site_id`, so
  device → site → customer follows;
* it populated at provisioning, and the router configured to present the
  matching identity;
* uniqueness enforced, since nothing in the AAA schema will.

C needs the customer's NAS set; A needs the site's. Both need this mapping. It
is schema and provisioning work, not a decision, and it is listed so the
implementation gate does not discover it late.
